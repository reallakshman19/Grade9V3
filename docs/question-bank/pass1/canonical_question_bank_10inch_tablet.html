Two equal masses m lie on a smooth horizontal surface and are joined by a light string of total length 2a. The midpoint of the initially taut string is pulled vertically upward by a small constant force F. When the masses are separated by 2x, determine the magnitude of the acceleration of either mass.

(A) (F/2m)·a/sqrt(a^2-x^2) (B) (F/2m)·x/sqrt(a^2-x^2) (C) (F/2m)·x/a (D) (F/2m)·sqrt(a^2-x^2)/x
Conditions: Horizontal surface is frictionless. String is light and remains taut. Each half-string has length a.
Chalkboard Visual Schematic • STRING_MIDPOINT_FBD MECHANICS_NLM
First Move Prompt

Sketch one half-string as a right triangle with horizontal projection x and vertical rise y.

Misconception / Common Wrong Route

Taking T=F/2 without resolving the angled string segments.

Rung 1 · Representation & Geometry
How does the vertical displacement y relate to half-string length a and half-separation x?
By Pythagoras, the vertical height is y = √(a² - x²), and cot θ = x / y = x / √(a² - x²).
Rung 2 · Governing Law (Massless Node)
At the pulled midpoint, which components of the two equal tensions balance F?
The midpoint is massless, so net vertical force must vanish: 2T sin θ = F ⇒ T = F / (2 sin θ).
Rung 3 · Crux Constraint (Horizontal Acceleration)
What horizontal force acts on either mass m, and how does Newton's second law determine its acceleration?
The only horizontal force on mass m is T cos θ: m a_x = T cos θ = [F / (2 sin θ)] cos θ = (F/2) cot θ ⇒ a_x = (F / 2m) [x / √(a² - x²)].
Step-Wise Solution (Canonical Teacher's Derivation) Mathematical Formulation
[Step 1]
Geometry of Half-String: y = √(a2 - x2),    cotθ = x / (√(a2 - x2)) • Decompose each half-string of length a. Horizontal displacement is x, vertical rise is y.
[Step 2]
Massless Knot Equilibrium: Σ Fy = F - 2T sinθ = 0 ⇒ T = F / 2sinθ = F a / (2√(a2 - x2)) • At the pulled midpoint, zero inertia requires instantaneous vertical balance.
[Step 3]
Newton II on Horizontal Mass: m · ax = T cosθ ⇒ ax = T cosθ / m • Only the horizontal string tension component accelerates each mass on the smooth surface.
[Step 4]
Algebraic Assembly & Resolution: ax = (F / 2m) x / (√(a2 - x2)) • Eliminating tension yields the exact closed form matching Option (B).
Answer & Independent Verification Check VERIFIED_CANONICAL

Option (B): a_mass = F x / [2m sqrt(a^2-x^2)].

As x approaches zero, the horizontal acceleration approaches zero because the string segments become nearly vertical.

Audit Note: Virtual work check: δy = -(x/y)δx; δW = F(-δy) - 2(m a_x)δx = 0 confirms identical acceleration. Limiting case x→0 gives a_x→0.

(1) the energy dissipated by friction before stopping is smaller on the incline; (2) the coefficient of friction becomes smaller when the plane is inclined. (A) Both true and (2) explains (1) (B) Both true but (2) does not explain (1) (C) (1) true, (2) false (D) (1) false, (2) true

First Move Prompt

Compare the destinations of the same initial kinetic energy in the horizontal and uphill cases.

Misconception / Common Wrong Route

Assuming a smaller friction force means the coefficient itself has changed.

Rung 1 · Work-Energy Accounting
Where does the initial kinetic energy go in each motion?
On the horizontal surface, 100% of kinetic energy is dissipated by friction. On the incline, part becomes potential energy.
Rung 2 · Normal Force & Friction Work
How does the normal force compare on the incline vs horizontal floor?
N_incline = mg cos θ < N_horiz = mg. Therefore frictional force is reduced along the incline.
Rung 3 · Coefficient Independence
Does the coefficient of friction μ depend on the tilt angle of the plane?
No, μ is an intrinsic material property of the contacting surfaces, completely independent of orientation.
Step-Wise Solution (Canonical Teacher's Derivation) Mathematical Formulation
[Step 1]
Horizontal Energy Dissipation: Wfric, horiz = Δ K = 1 / 2 m v02 • On a horizontal plane, all initial kinetic energy is dissipated by friction before stopping.
[Step 2]
Incline Energy Balance: Wfric, inc + m g h = 1 / 2 m v02 ⇒ Wfric, inc < 1 / 2 m v02 • Going up the incline, part of the initial kinetic energy is converted into gravitational potential energy mgh.
[Step 3]
Surface Property Invariance: μ = constant (independent of θ) • Tilting changes the normal reaction N = mg cos θ, not the surface coefficient μ. Thus Statement 1 is true, Statement 2 is false (Option C).
Answer & Independent Verification Check VERIFIED_CANONICAL

Option (C): statement (1) is true and statement (2) is false.

The incline case satisfies K_initial = gain in gravitational potential energy + energy dissipated by friction.

Audit Note: Limiting case θ→90°: vertical throw has zero normal force and zero friction work; all kinetic energy converts to potential energy.

Pulling exerts an upward vertical component F sin θ; pushing exerts a downward component -F sin θ.

Step-Wise Solution (Canonical Teacher's Derivation) Mathematical Formulation

Audit Note: Limiting case θ→0: If the applied force is purely horizontal, N_pull = N_push = mg, and the distinction between pulling and pushing disappears.

A block is on a plane inclined at 45 degrees. The coefficient of friction is mu. The force needed to just push the block upward is three times the force needed to just prevent it from sliding downward. If N = 10 mu, find N.

Conditions: Both forces act parallel to the incline. Each case is at limiting equilibrium.
Chalkboard Visual Schematic • INCLINE_FRICTION_FBD MECHANICS_NLM
First Move Prompt

Draw two separate free-body diagrams for impending upward and impending downward motion.

Misconception / Common Wrong Route

Using the same friction direction in both limiting cases.

Rung 1 · Impending Motion FBDs
In which direction does friction act when impending motion is upward vs downward?
When impending motion is up the plane, friction opposes upward motion (down the plane). When preventing slipping down, friction acts up the plane.
Rung 2 · Force Balance Formulations
Formulate F1 (just move up) and F2 (just prevent sliding)?
F1 = mg(sin 45° + μ cos 45°) and F2 = mg(sin 45° - μ cos 45°).
Rung 3 · Ratio Coupling
How does F1 = 3 F2 determine μ and N = 10μ?
(1 + μ) / (1 - μ) = 3 ⇒ 1 + μ = 3 - 3μ ⇒ 4μ = 2 ⇒ μ = 0.5. Thus N = 10μ = 5.
Step-Wise Solution (Canonical Teacher's Derivation) Mathematical Formulation
[Step 1]
Impending Motion FBDs: m g sin 45^° = m g cos 45^° = m g / √(2),    N = m g / √(2) • At θ = 45°, gravitational components parallel and perpendicular to the incline are identical.
[Step 2]
Upward Limiting Force F1: F1 = m g(sin 45^° + μ cos 45^°) = m g / √(2)(1 + μ) • To move the block up, applied force must overcome both down-plane gravity and down-plane static friction.
[Step 3]
Downward Preventing Force F2: F2 = m g(sin 45^° - μ cos 45^°) = m g / √(2)(1 - μ) • To prevent downward slipping, friction acts up the plane assisting the applied force.
[Step 4]
Ratio Coupling & Integer Evaluation: F1 / F2 = (1 + μ) / (1 - μ) = 3 ⇒ 1 + μ = 3 - 3μ ⇒ μ = 0.5,    N = 10μ = 5 • Solving the linear ratio yields μ = 0.5, and requested integer N = 10μ = 5.
Answer & Independent Verification Check VERIFIED_CANONICAL

5.

With mu=1/2, the two force factors are 1.5 and 0.5, a 3:1 ratio.

Audit Note: Back-substitution: With μ = 0.5, (1 + 0.5) / (1 - 0.5) = 1.5 / 0.5 = 3.0, perfectly satisfying the 3:1 ratio.
