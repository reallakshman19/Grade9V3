🔍 Diagnostic Learning Arc: 4 Fundamental Inferential Cruxes

Mastery in polynomials requires navigating beyond superficial substitution. This clinical suite trains the 4 pivotal conceptual jumps defined in the unit syllabus:

1. Structural Recognition

Distinguish variable exponents from constant exponents; identify degree after full simplification and term cancellation.

2. Divisibility & Reconstruction

Treat remainders as evaluatable quantities; reconstruct dividends directly via Division Algorithm without blind long division.

3. Symmetrical Branching

Preserve dual square-root branches ($\pm$) in symmetric identity transformations; avoid premature sign collapse.

4. Sign-Sensitive Expansion

Track distributive subtractions across mirrored cubic expansions and Sophie Germain completions.

Q1 · Constant Polynomial Degree SRC-POLY-M2-FIB-01 · D1 (Direct Recognition)
$\sqrt{2}$ is a polynomial of degree _____.
💡 Progressive 3-Rung Hint Ladder (Click to Expand)
Rung 1 (ORIENT)A non-zero real constant number can be rewritten with an indeterminate $x$ raised to a power that equals 1.
Rung 2 (FIRST_RELATION)Express $\sqrt{2}$ in variable form: $\sqrt{2} = \sqrt{2} \cdot x^n$. For $x eq 0$, what non-negative integer satisfies $x^n = 1$?
Rung 3 (CRUX_MOVE)Since $x^0 = 1$, we have $\sqrt{2} = \sqrt{2}x^0$. The highest variable power is 0. Therefore the degree is 0.
🧭 Formal Reasoning Route:
REPRESENTATION_CONVERSIONExpress constant in algebraic form: $\sqrt{2} = \sqrt{2} \cdot x^0$
Why Valid: For any real indeterminate $x eq 0$, $x^0 = 1$.
DEGREE_AXIOM_APPLICATIONBy standard definition (NCERT §2.1), degree of any non-zero constant polynomial is 0
Why Valid: Highest power of variable with non-zero coefficient is 0. Note zero polynomial 0 has undefined degree.
Constant √2 has degree 0. The zero polynomial 0 has no defined degree.
Q2 · Targeted Cross-Term Expansion SRC-POLY-M2-FIB-03 · D2 (Connected Reasoning)
The coefficient of $x^2$ in the product $(3x^2 - 5)(4 + 4x^2)$ is _____.
💡 Progressive 3-Rung Hint Ladder (Click to Expand)
Rung 1 (ORIENT)Do not expand all 4 terms. Only combine terms from the first bracket and second bracket that multiply to produce $x^2$.
Rung 2 (FIRST_RELATION)$(3x^2) imes 4 = 12x^2$ and $(-5) imes (4x^2) = -20x^2$.
Rung 3 (CRUX_MOVE)Sum the two coefficients: $12 + (-20) = -8$. The coefficient of $x^2$ is $-8$.
🧭 Formal Reasoning Route:
TARGETED_CROSS_PRODUCTIdentify terms yielding exponent 2: $(3x^2)(4) + (-5)(4x^2) = 12x^2 - 20x^2$
Why Valid: Exponents add: $2+0=2$ and $0+2=2$. Terms like $(3x^2)(4x^2)=12x^4$ do not affect $x^2$.
LIKE_TERM_COMBINATION$12 - 20 = -8$, therefore the coefficient is $-8$
Why Valid: Combining like coefficients over the distributive property.
Only the anti-diagonal produces $x^2$ powers: $12 - 20 = -8$.
Q3 · Degree Classification SRC-POLY-M2-SC-10 · D1 (Direct Recognition)
Which of the following is a cubic polynomial?
💡 Progressive 3-Rung Hint Ladder (Click to Expand)
Rung 1 (ORIENT)A cubic polynomial is defined as a polynomial of degree 3.
Rung 2 (FIRST_RELATION)Examine the highest non-zero power of $x$ across all 4 choices.
Rung 3 (CRUX_MOVE)Only choice (A) has an $x^3$ term (degree 3). B, C, and D are quadratic polynomials (degree 2).
🧭 Formal Reasoning Route:
DEGREE_DEFINITIONA cubic polynomial has degree 3: $\max \{k : c_k eq 0\} = 3$
Why Valid: Standard nomenclature: 1=linear, 2=quadratic, 3=cubic, 4=quartic.
CANDIDATE_DISCRIMINATION(A) has leading term $1 \cdot x^3$; (B), (C), (D) have highest term $x^2$. Thus Option A is unique.
Why Valid: (D) expands to $3x^2 + 3x + 3$, which remains quadratic.
Option (A) is the sole polynomial with degree 3.
Q4 · Assertion & Reason SRC-POLY-M2-AR-03 · D2 (Connected Reasoning)
Assertion (A): $3\sqrt{y} + 4$ is not a polynomial.
Reason (R): In a polynomial, the exponent of each variable must be a non-negative integer (whole number).
💡 Progressive 3-Rung Hint Ladder (Click to Expand)
Rung 1 (ORIENT)Rewrite $\sqrt{y}$ in exponent form: $\sqrt{y} = y^{1/2}$.
Rung 2 (FIRST_RELATION)Check whether $1/2$ is a whole number (non-negative integer). Is $1/2 \in \{0, 1, 2, \dots\}$?
Rung 3 (CRUX_MOVE)$1/2$ is fractional, violating the definition of a polynomial. Reason R gives the exact rule why Assertion A holds. Option A is correct.
🧭 Formal Reasoning Route:
EXPONENT_DOMAIN_CHECK$\sqrt{y} = y^{1/2}$ has exponent $1/2 otin \mathbb{N}_0$
Why Valid: Exponents must be non-negative integers. Fractional exponents produce algebraic irrationalities.
CAUSAL_SUFFICIENCY_CHECKReason R states the exact formal condition that invalidates Assertion A, establishing causal correctness
Why Valid: A is true because of R.
Variable exponents must be whole numbers: $1/2 otin \{0, 1, 2, \dots\}$.
Q5 · Constant Exponent Trap SRC-POLY-M2-VSA-01 · D2 (Connected Reasoning)
What is the degree of the polynomial $2^5$?
💡 Progressive 3-Rung Hint Ladder (Click to Expand)
Rung 1 (ORIENT)Is 5 the power of a variable $x$, or is it the power of the constant number 2?
Rung 2 (FIRST_RELATION)$2^5 = 32$. The polynomial is simply the constant number 32.
Rung 3 (CRUX_MOVE)Write $32 = 32x^0$. The degree is determined solely by the power on the variable ($x$), not on the coefficient. The degree is 0.
🧭 Formal Reasoning Route:
BASE_NUMERICAL_COLLAPSE$2^5 = 32 \in \mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$
Why Valid: Exponent is an internal property of numerical representation, not a polynomial indeterminate.
INDETERMINATE_POWER_INSPECTION$32 = 32x^0 \implies \deg(2^5) = 0$
Why Valid: By definition, degree is the highest exponent of the variable with non-zero coefficient.
The exponent 5 belongs to the constant base 2, not to variable $x$.
Q6 · Binomial Square Expansion SRC-POLY-M2-NUM-14 · D1 (Direct Application)
Find the coefficient of $x$ in the expansion of $(x - 4)^2$.
💡 Progressive 3-Rung Hint Ladder (Click to Expand)
Rung 1 (ORIENT)Use the identity $(a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2$.
Rung 2 (FIRST_RELATION)With $a = x$ and $b = 4$, the middle term is $-2(x)(4) = -8x$.
Rung 3 (CRUX_MOVE)The coefficient of $x$ is $-8$. Remember to keep the minus sign!
🧭 Formal Reasoning Route:
IDENTITY_SUBSTITUTION$(x - 4)^2 = x^2 - 2(4)x + 4^2 = x^2 - 8x + 16$
Why Valid: Exact algebraic identity for difference of two terms.
LINEAR_COEFFICIENT_EXTRACTIONTerm is $-8x \implies ext{coefficient} = -8$
Why Valid: Coefficient includes the sign preceding the numeral.
Geometric area decomposition: two rectangular slices give $-4x - 4x = -8x$.
Q7 · Coefficient Cancellation Check SRC-POLY-M2-ARC-13 · D2 (Connected Reasoning)
The degree of the polynomial $3x^2 + 12 - (\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{2})x^2 + 7x$ is:
💡 Progressive 3-Rung Hint Ladder (Click to Expand)
Rung 1 (ORIENT)Group the $x^2$ terms: $[3 - (\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{2})]x^2 + 7x + 12$.
Rung 2 (FIRST_RELATION)Does $3 - (\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{2})$ equal zero? Note $\sqrt{3} pprox 1.732$ and $\sqrt{2} pprox 1.414$, so $\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{2} pprox 3.146 eq 3$.
Rung 3 (CRUX_MOVE)Because the coefficient is non-zero, the $x^2$ term survives. The highest surviving exponent is 2. The degree is 2 (Option C).
🧭 Formal Reasoning Route:
LIKE_TERM_CANONICALIZATIONCombine $x^2$ terms: $[3 - (\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{2})]x^2 + 7x + 12$
Why Valid: Terms with matching variable powers combine by distributivity.
IRRATIONAL_SUM_EVALUATION$\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{2} pprox 3.146 \implies 3 - (\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{2}) eq 0$
Why Valid: If $3 = \sqrt{3}+\sqrt{2}$, then squaring gives $9 = 5 + 2\sqrt{6} \implies \sqrt{6} = 2$, contradiction ($6 eq 4$). Coefficient is strictly non-zero.
The coefficient of $x^2$ is non-zero, so the degree remains 2.
Q8 · Dual Remainder Theorem SRC-POLY-M2-HOTS-16 · D3 (LP-H3 Target)
The polynomials $P_1(x) = ax^3 + 3x^2 - 3$ and $P_2(x) = 2x^3 - 5x + a$ when divided by $(x - 4)$ leave remainders $R_1$ and $R_2$ respectively. Find the value of $a$ if $2R_1 - R_2 = 0$.
💡 Progressive 3-Rung Hint Ladder (Click to Expand)
Rung 1 (ORIENT)By the Remainder Theorem, $R_1 = P_1(4)$ and $R_2 = P_2(4)$. Evaluate both remainders as linear expressions in $a$.
Rung 2 (FIRST_RELATION)$R_1 = a(4^3) + 3(4^2) - 3 = 64a + 48 - 3 = 64a + 45$.
$R_2 = 2(4^3) - 5(4) + a = 128 - 20 + a = 108 + a$.
Rung 3 (CRUX_MOVE)Impose $2R_1 - R_2 = 0 \implies 2(64a + 45) - (108 + a) = 0 \implies 128a + 90 - 108 - a = 0 \implies 127a = 18 \implies a = 18/127$.
🧭 Formal Reasoning Route:
REMAINDER_THEOREM_DUAL_EVALUATION$R_1 = P_1(4) = 64a + 45$ and $R_2 = P_2(4) = a + 108$
Why Valid: Remainder when dividing $P(x)$ by $x-c$ is identically $P(c)$.
LINEAR_RELATION_SYNTHESIS$2(64a + 45) - (a + 108) = 127a - 18 = 0 \implies a = rac{18}{127}$
Why Valid: Directly satisfies the coupled remainder constraint.
Dual remainder constraint model: solve for $a$ without trial and error.
Q9 · Reverse Division Reconstruction SRC-POLY-M2-ARC-22 · D3 (LP-H3 Target)
When polynomial $p(x)$ is divided by $(x^2 - 1)$, the quotient is $(x + 2)$ and remainder is $(2x + 1)$. Reconstruct the dividend polynomial $p(x)$:
💡 Progressive 3-Rung Hint Ladder (Click to Expand)
Rung 1 (ORIENT)Apply the Division Algorithm directly: $ ext{Dividend} = ext{Divisor} imes ext{Quotient} + ext{Remainder}$.
Rung 2 (FIRST_RELATION)Multiply divisor by quotient: $(x^2 - 1)(x + 2) = x^3 + 2x^2 - x - 2$.
Rung 3 (CRUX_MOVE)Add the remainder $(2x + 1)$: $(x^3 + 2x^2 - x - 2) + (2x + 1) = x^3 + 2x^2 + (-x + 2x) + (-2 + 1) = x^3 + 2x^2 + x - 1$. Matches Option A.
🧭 Formal Reasoning Route:
DIVISION_ALGORITHM_RECONSTRUCTION$p(x) = (x^2 - 1)(x + 2) + (2x + 1)$
Why Valid: Fundamental theorem of polynomial arithmetic: $p(x) = d(x)q(x) + r(x)$ with $\deg(r) < \deg(d)$.
ALGEBRAIC_CLOSURE$x^3 + 2x^2 - x - 2 + 2x + 1 = x^3 + 2x^2 + x - 1$ (Option A)
Why Valid: Exact distributive synthesis avoids long division. (Note: Printed book key had typographical error; Option A is verified correct).
Reverse division algorithm: reconstruct $p(x) = d(x)q(x) + r(x)$.
Q10 · Symmetrical Branching & Sign Loss SRC-POLY-M2-MC-21 · D3 (LP-H4 Target)
If $x - y = 5$ and $xy = 24$, which of the following is/are correct?
(I) $(x + y)^2 = 121$
(II) $x + y = \pm 11$
(III) $x^3 - y^3 = 485$
💡 Progressive 3-Rung Hint Ladder (Click to Expand)
Rung 1 (ORIENT)Use the connecting identity: $(x + y)^2 = (x - y)^2 + 4xy$.
Rung 2 (FIRST_RELATION)$(x + y)^2 = 5^2 + 4(24) = 25 + 96 = 121$. Taking square roots gives two branches: $x + y = \pm 11$. (Do not drop the negative branch!)
Rung 3 (CRUX_MOVE)For $x^3 - y^3$, apply $(x - y)^3 + 3xy(x - y) = 5^3 + 3(24)(5) = 125 + 360 = 485$. All three statements are mathematically correct (Option D).
🧭 Formal Reasoning Route:
SYMMETRIC_SUM_IDENTITY$(x+y)^2 = (x-y)^2 + 4xy = 25 + 96 = 121 \implies x+y = \pm 11$
Why Valid: Squaring loses sign; extracting principal and negative roots produces two valid real branches.
CUBIC_DIFFERENCE_EVALUATION$x^3 - y^3 = (x-y)[(x-y)^2 + 3xy] = 5[25 + 72] = 5 imes 97 = 485$
Why Valid: Purely determined by $(x-y)$ and $xy$; single-valued at 485.
Dual-branch sign preservation: $(x+y)^2 = 121 \implies x+y = \pm 11$.
Q11 · Sign Preservation in Cubic Subtraction SRC-POLY-M2-LA-07 · D3 (LP-H4 Target)
Simplify $(2x - 5y)^3 - (2x + 5y)^3$ completely:
💡 Progressive 3-Rung Hint Ladder (Click to Expand)
Rung 1 (ORIENT)Notice the form $(A - B)^3 - (A + B)^3$. Expand both using the binomial theorem or cubic identities.
Rung 2 (FIRST_RELATION)$(A - B)^3 = A^3 - 3A^2B + 3AB^2 - B^3$.
$(A + B)^3 = A^3 + 3A^2B + 3AB^2 + B^3$.
Subtracting the second from the first gives $-6A^2B - 2B^3$.
Rung 3 (CRUX_MOVE)Substitute $A = 2x, B = 5y$: $-6(2x)^2(5y) - 2(5y)^3 = -6(4x^2)(5y) - 2(125y^3) = -120x^2y - 250y^3$. Watch the overall negative sign (Option B).
🧭 Formal Reasoning Route:
MIRRORED_CUBIC_EXPANSION$(A-B)^3 - (A+B)^3 = -2(3A^2B + B^3) = -6A^2B - 2B^3$
Why Valid: Terms with even powers of $B$ cancel: $A^3 - A^3 = 0$ and $3AB^2 - 3AB^2 = 0$.
OUTER_SIGN_PRESERVATION$-6(4x^2)(5y) - 2(125y^3) = -120x^2y - 250y^3$ (Option B)
Why Valid: Preserves overall negation; book printed key incorrectly omitted the minus sign.
Even powers of $B$ cancel; odd powers double with a strict negative sign.
Q12 · Sophie Germain Square Completion SRC-POLY-M2-HOTS-01 · D4 (Olympiad Reduction)
Factorize completely: $x^4 + x^2 + 1$.
💡 Progressive 3-Rung Hint Ladder (Click to Expand)
Rung 1 (ORIENT)Notice that $(x^2 + 1)^2 = x^4 + 2x^2 + 1$. Our expression has $x^2$ instead of $2x^2$.
Rung 2 (FIRST_RELATION)Add and subtract $x^2$: $x^4 + x^2 + 1 = (x^4 + 2x^2 + 1) - x^2 = (x^2 + 1)^2 - x^2$.
Rung 3 (CRUX_MOVE)Apply difference of squares $A^2 - B^2 = (A - B)(A + B)$ with $A = x^2 + 1$ and $B = x$: $(x^2 + 1 - x)(x^2 + 1 + x) = (x^2 - x + 1)(x^2 + x + 1)$. Matches Option A.
🧭 Formal Reasoning Route:
ALGEBRAIC_SQUARE_COMPLETION$x^4 + x^2 + 1 = (x^4 + 2x^2 + 1) - x^2 = (x^2 + 1)^2 - x^2$
Why Valid: Adding and subtracting identical non-zero quantity preserves algebraic value.
DIFFERENCE_OF_SQUARES_FACTORIZATION$(x^2 + 1)^2 - (x)^2 = (x^2 - x + 1)(x^2 + x + 1)$
Why Valid: Irreducible quadratic factors over real numbers $\mathbb{R}$.
Complete the square $(x^2+1)^2$, then subtract $(x)^2$ to factorize.
