Horse–Cart Paradox

Question

If the horse pulls the cart and the cart pulls the horse equally hard in the opposite direction, why can the pair accelerate?

Wrong shortcut: “Horse-on-cart and cart-on-horse are equal and opposite, so they cancel and motion is impossible.”
Third-law rule: each interaction pair has equal magnitude and opposite direction, but the two forces act on different bodies.
horse on cart = +T    |    cart on horse = -T
horse on ground = -Fg    |    ground on horse = +Fg
Choose the system before cancelling anything. For the combined horse+cart system, the horse–cart pair is internal. The ground-on-horse traction is external.
ΣFexternal = Fg = (mh + mc)a
Resolution: the pair accelerates because the environment exerts a non-zero external force on the chosen horse+cart system.

What this explorer is protecting
  • Third-law partners belong to different receivers.
  • Internal-force cancellation is a system-boundary operation, not a claim that the forces disappear.
  • The horse moves forward because the ground pushes the horse forward; the horse–cart interaction redistributes that external effect between the two bodies.
  • If the external horizontal force were zero in this idealized model, the combined-system acceleration would be zero.
