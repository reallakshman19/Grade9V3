Q-PHY-KIN-2D-EXT-2B-VELOCITY-DIRECTION

Prior exposure: Q-PHY-KIN-2D-EXT-2A-VELOCITY-DIRECTION

Changed demand: Both component position laws now vary with time; the learner must independently choose differentiation as the representation before any angle calculation.

A particle follows x=2t²+3t and y=12t-t². At t=1 s, decide the direction of motion. Neither velocity component is constant.

Safe pre-attempt support: List what changed from the cited Core2A parent and name the information you still need to decide.

Post-attempt reasoning

  1. REPRESENT — List x(t), y(t) and the requested instant without selecting a direction formula.
    This keeps the decision open.
  2. DECIDE — Choose derivative components, not position ratios, to represent instantaneous direction.
    The demand asks for velocity direction. protected move
  3. TRANSFORM — Evaluate v=(7,10) and obtain the angle.
    Both components use t=1.
  4. VERIFY — Check tangent consistency.
    Velocity is tangent.

Answer: At t=1 s, v_x=7 and v_y=10 m/s, so theta=tan^-1(10/7) above +x.

Check: A position-vector angle would be a different quantity.

Rubric

  • Selects the model or representation before calculating. — The governing decision is explicit and justified.
  • Carries the reasoning to a checked result. — The solution closes without tutor input.

Q-PHY-KIN-2D-EXT-2B-BOUNCE

Prior exposure: Q-PHY-KIN-2D-EXT-2A-BOUNCE

Changed demand: The rebound direction is supplied as a component ratio rather than an angle; the learner must still protect the energy-to-speed-squared decision before translating the new representation.

A projectile returns to level ground with speed U. After impact it retains 36% of its kinetic energy, and its rebound velocity has vertical:horizontal component ratio 3:4. Find its next maximum height in terms of U and g.

Safe pre-attempt support: List what changed from the cited Core2A parent and name the information you still need to decide.

Post-attempt reasoning

  1. REPRESENT — List retained energy and the 3:4 direction information separately.
    This does not hand over how energy maps to speed.
  2. DECIDE — Convert the energy fraction to a speed-squared fraction before using the component ratio.
    K is proportional to v². protected move
  3. TRANSFORM — Use the 3:4 ratio to get v_y² and then height.
    A 3-4-5 ratio sets the vertical speed fraction.
  4. VERIFY — Check scaling against retained energy.
    Height cannot exceed retained-energy vertical bound.

Answer: H=81U²/(1250g).

Check: The result uses both the energy fraction and component-ratio squared, so it is much less than U²/(2g).

Rubric

  • Selects the model or representation before calculating. — The governing decision is explicit and justified.
  • Carries the reasoning to a checked result. — The solution closes without tutor input.

Q-PHY-KIN-2D-EXT-2B-PIECEWISE-G

Prior exposure: Q-PHY-KIN-2D-EXT-2A-PIECEWISE-G

Changed demand: The apex state is supplied directly and the target is only the second-stage displacement; the learner must decide to start from the boundary rather than reuse whole-flight symmetry.

A projectile reaches its apex at horizontal position L and height H with horizontal speed V. Immediately after the apex, downward gravity changes to g/4. Decide the additional horizontal distance before it reaches the launch level.

Safe pre-attempt support: List what changed from the cited Core2A parent and name the information you still need to decide.

Post-attempt reasoning

  1. REPRESENT — Record the given apex state and the post-apex gravity.
    The ascent result is already supplied.
  2. DECIDE — Use the supplied apex as the initial state of a new constant-acceleration stage.
    The model changes only after that event. protected move
  3. TRANSFORM — Solve fall time under g/4 and multiply by V.
    Horizontal speed remains unchanged.
  4. VERIFY — Compare with the ordinary-g descent.
    Weaker gravity must increase distance.

Answer: Additional distance = 2V sqrt(2H/g).

Check: Weaker gravity lengthens the descent relative to ordinary g.

  • Selects the model or representation before calculating. — The governing decision is explicit and justified.

Q-PHY-KIN-2D-EXT-2B-POINT

Prior exposure: Q-PHY-KIN-2D-EXT-2A-POINT

Changed demand: Instead of solving for speed after point substitution, the learner must recognize a reachability boundary from the sign of the gravity term.

From the origin, can a finite-speed gravity-only projectile launched exactly along the straight line joining the origin to P=(5 m,1 m) pass through P? Justify without solving for a numerical speed.

Safe pre-attempt support: List what changed from the cited Core2A parent and name the information you still need to decide.

Post-attempt reasoning

  1. REPRESENT — Plot the straight launch ray through P and the gravity-only trajectory form without deciding reachability.
    This exposes the changed representation but not the key comparison.
  2. DECIDE — Compare P with the straight-line term before attempting to solve for u.
    The gravity term always lowers the finite-speed path. protected move
  3. CONNECT — Show the required y already equals x tanθ, leaving no room for the negative gravity term.
    For finite u the term is strictly negative.
  4. VERIFY — Inspect u→∞ as a limit.
    The path approaches but never equals the ray at finite u.

Answer: No finite speed can make it pass through P.

Check: In the infinite-speed limit the gravity term tends to zero, showing why no finite speed reaches the exact point on that ray.

  • Selects the model or representation before calculating. — The governing decision is explicit and justified.
  • Carries the reasoning to a checked result. — The solution closes without tutor input.

Q-PHY-KIN-2D-EXT-2B-DRAG

Prior exposure: Q-PHY-KIN-2D-EXT-2A-DRAG

Changed demand: The drag parameter is given through a velocity half-life rather than c/m; the learner must recognize and translate the exponential representation before integrating.

A projectile's measured horizontal velocity halves every τ seconds in a medium. Decide whether x=v_x0 t is valid and write a horizontal displacement expression in terms of v_x0, τ and t consistent with linear drag.

Safe pre-attempt support: List what changed from the cited Core2A parent and name the information you still need to decide.

Post-attempt reasoning

  1. REPRESENT — Record the empirical half-life statement without assuming constant horizontal velocity.
    A halving law is incompatible with v_x constant.
  2. DECIDE — Translate the halving statement into an exponential velocity law before computing distance.
    Linear drag produces exponential velocity decay. protected move
  3. TRANSFORM — Integrate the exponential law.
    Displacement is the time integral of velocity.
  4. VERIFY — Take the short-time limit.
    It approaches v_x0 t.

Answer: x=(v_x0 τ/ln2)[1-2^{-t/τ}], so x=v_x0 t is not valid except as a short-time/zero-drag limit.

Check: At small t the expression expands to approximately v_x0 t.

Rubric

  • Selects the model or representation before calculating. — The governing decision is explicit and justified.
  • Carries the reasoning to a checked result. — The solution closes without tutor input.
