AUTHORED_PRACTICEQ-PHY-KIN-2D-EXT-2A-VELOCITY-DIRECTIONA particle has x(t)=10t and y(t)=18t-2t^2 in metres. Find the direction of its instantaneous velocity at t=2 s.The velocity components at 2 s are (10,10) m/s, so the direction is 45° above +x.
AUTHORED_PRACTICEQ-PHY-KIN-2D-EXT-2A-BOUNCEA projectile launched at 45° reaches 80 m maximum height. On returning to the ground it retains 60% of its kinetic energy and rebounds at 30° above horizontal. Find its new maximum height.The new maximum height is 24 m.
AUTHORED_PRACTICEQ-PHY-KIN-2D-EXT-2A-PIECEWISE-GAn ordinary projectile would have range R. At the apex it enters a region where downward gravity becomes 4g while horizontal velocity is unchanged. Express the new range as a fraction of R.The new range is 3R/4.
AUTHORED_PRACTICEQ-PHY-KIN-2D-EXT-2A-POINTA projectile launched at 45° passes through P=(4 m,1 m). Find u²/g and decide whether the projectile is rising or falling at P.u²/g=16/3 m and the projectile is already descending at P.
AUTHORED_PRACTICEQ-PHY-KIN-2D-EXT-2A-DRAGA projectile has horizontal launch component 120 m/s. In a medium with linear drag, m/c=2 s. Find its horizontal displacement after 2 s using e≈2.718.The displacement is about 152 m.
AUTHORED_PRACTICEQ-PHY-KIN-2D-EXT-2B-VELOCITY-DIRECTIONA particle follows x=2t²+3t and y=12t-t². At t=1 s, decide the direction of motion. Neither velocity component is constant.At t=1 s, v_x=7 and v_y=10 m/s, so theta=tan^-1(10/7) above +x.
AUTHORED_PRACTICEQ-PHY-KIN-2D-EXT-2B-BOUNCEA projectile returns to level ground with speed U. After impact it retains 36% of its kinetic energy, and its rebound velocity has vertical:horizontal component ratio 3:4. Find its next maximum height in terms of U and g.H=81U²/(1250g).
AUTHORED_PRACTICEQ-PHY-KIN-2D-EXT-2B-PIECEWISE-GA projectile reaches its apex at horizontal position L and height H with horizontal speed V. Immediately after the apex, downward gravity changes to g/4. Decide the additional horizontal distance before it reaches the launch level.Additional distance = 2V sqrt(2H/g).
AUTHORED_PRACTICEQ-PHY-KIN-2D-EXT-2B-POINTFrom the origin, can a finite-speed gravity-only projectile launched exactly along the straight line joining the origin to P=(5 m,1 m) pass through P? Justify without solving for a numerical speed.No finite speed can make it pass through P.
AUTHORED_PRACTICEQ-PHY-KIN-2D-EXT-2B-DRAGA projectile's measured horizontal velocity halves every τ seconds in a medium. Decide whether x=v_x0 t is valid and write a horizontal displacement expression in terms of v_x0, τ and t consistent with linear drag.x=(v_x0 τ/ln2)[1-2^{-t/τ}], so x=v_x0 t is not valid except as a short-time/zero-drag limit.
