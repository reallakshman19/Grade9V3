Concept Book

Redox Reactions

Learning promise

Track oxidation-state change, identify oxidising and reducing roles, classify redox patterns, and balance a stated ionic reaction with atom and charge checks.

Concept route

  1. Oxidation number, oxidation/reduction and agents
    1. Oxidation number is a formal assignment whose increase corresponds to oxidation and decrease to reduction; an oxidising agent is itself reduced while a reducing agent is itself oxidised.
  2. Redox classification, displacement and disproportionation patterns
    1. A reaction is redox only when oxidation-number bookkeeping shows coupled increase and decrease; reaction labels such as decomposition or displacement do not replace that test.
  3. Half-reaction balancing and reaction-dependent n-factor
    1. Half-reaction balancing is a conservation algorithm, while n-factor compresses the electron change for one specified redox transformation; neither is valid without the reaction context.

Model contract

  • Treat oxidation number as formal electron bookkeeping unless a separate physical-charge claim is supported.
  • Identify oxidation and reduction before naming oxidising and reducing agents.
  • State acidic or basic aqueous medium before introducing H+, OH- or H2O during half-reaction balancing.

The key step

Oxidation number is a formal assignment whose increase corresponds to oxidation and decrease to reduction; an oxidising agent is itself reduced while a reducing agent is itself oxidised.

You need

  • Can assign simple oxidation numbers using standard priority rules and can track the same element from reactants to products.
  1. Apply oxidation-number priority rules and require the algebraic sum to equal the species charge.
    Why valid: The assignment is a formal bookkeeping convention constrained by overall charge.
    Result: Charge reconciliation closes the oxidation-number ledger.
  2. Track the same element from reactant to product and classify increases as oxidation and decreases as reduction.
    Why valid: A direction can only be named after matching the element across both sides.
    Result: Electron-loss/gain and oxidation-number change are two views of the same bookkeeping.
  3. Name the species being reduced as the oxidising agent and the species being oxidised as the reducing agent.
    Why valid: Agent names describe the process caused in the partner, not the self-change.
    Result: Separating self-change from agent role prevents reversal.

Equations and validity

EquationWhat it tells youWhen you can use it

Σ ni(ONi) = species charge

Oxidation numbers are formal assignments constrained by the total species charge; an increase tracks oxidation and a decrease tracks reduction.

  • Apply the oxidation-number priority rules consistently.
  • The algebraic weighted sum must equal the charge of the whole species.

Watch one

Species A gains electrons from species B. Which species is the oxidising agent and why?

  1. A gains electrons, so A undergoes reduction.
  2. The electrons come from B, so B is oxidised.
  3. The species causing oxidation of its partner is the oxidising agent.

Result

Species A is the oxidising agent because A is reduced while causing B to be oxidised.

Check

The oxidising agent must appear on the reduction side of the bookkeeping.

Oxidation number is a formal assignment whose increase corresponds to oxidation and decrease to reduction; an oxidising agent is itself reduced while a reducing agent is itself oxidised.

A tempting wrong path

  • Oxidation number is a literal measured atomic charge, and the species oxidised is the oxidising agent.

Diagnose

  • Can resonance/electron sharing make physical charge differ from formal oxidation number, and what happens to the electron acceptor itself?

Repair

  • Label oxidation state as formal bookkeeping; write self-change first, then assign the opposite process as the agent name.

  1. 1 · CheckIf a species is the oxidising agent, what happens to that species itself?
  2. 2 · ApplyIn Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu, identify what is oxidised, what is reduced, and both agents.
  3. 3 · ConnectUse the same before/after oxidation-state ledger to classify whole reaction patterns.

Redox classification, displacement and disproportionation patterns

The key step

A reaction is redox only when oxidation-number bookkeeping shows coupled increase and decrease; reaction labels such as decomposition or displacement do not replace that test.

You need

  • Can compare oxidation numbers for the same element on both sides of a reaction and can read reactant-to-product direction explicitly.
  1. Recognise the chemical reaction without yet calling it redox.
    Why valid: Formation of new substances is not sufficient evidence of electron-transfer bookkeeping.
    Result: Chemical-change classification and redox classification are separate questions.
  2. Compare oxidation numbers on both sides and require at least one increase coupled to a decrease.
    Why valid: A double displacement can be non-redox, while a displacement can be redox depending on actual state changes.
    Result: Oxidation-state change is the deciding test.
  3. For the same element, map one initial state splitting upward and downward as disproportionation, and two starting states converging as comproportionation.
    Why valid: The classification depends on the directed oxidation-state map.
    Result: Counting how many states appear is not enough.

Equations and validity

EquationWhat it tells youWhen you can use it

redox ⇔ at least one ON increases and at least one ON decreases

A reaction is redox when oxidation-number changes are coupled; labels such as decomposition or displacement do not establish redox by themselves.

  • Track the same element from reactant to product species.
  • Use actual before/after oxidation numbers rather than a reaction-name shortcut.

Watch one

The same element begins in one oxidation state and appears in two products, one at a higher and one at a lower oxidation state. What pattern is this?

  1. One starting oxidation state is both oxidised and reduced in different product portions.
  2. The state map branches upward and downward from the same initial value.

Result

Disproportionation.

Check

Both an increase and a decrease must originate from the same initial oxidation state.

A reaction is redox only when oxidation-number bookkeeping shows coupled increase and decrease; reaction labels such as decomposition or displacement do not replace that test.

A tempting wrong path

  • Every chemical reaction is redox, and any reaction showing three oxidation states is disproportionation.

Diagnose

  • Can ions exchange partners with unchanged oxidation numbers, and are the three states arranged as one-to-two or two-to-one across the reaction arrow?

Repair

  • Run the oxidation-number change check first, then draw the directed state map before naming a special redox pattern.

1-2-3 quick check

  1. 1 · CheckWhat oxidation-state evidence is required before calling a reaction redox?
  2. 2 · ApplyIn Cl₂ + 2OH⁻ → Cl⁻ + ClO⁻ + H₂O, chlorine goes 0 → −1 and 0 → +1. Classify the pattern.
  3. 3 · ConnectSplit the two directions of oxidation-state change into half-reactions before balancing.

The key step

Half-reaction balancing is a conservation algorithm, while n-factor compresses the electron change for one specified redox transformation; neither is valid without the reaction context.

You need

  • Can conserve atom counts and algebraic charge in an ionic equation and can interpret electrons as transfer bookkeeping.
  1. In acidic solution, write and balance the permanganate reduction half-reaction: MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O.
    Why valid: O is balanced with H₂O, H with H⁺, and charge with electrons; both atoms and charge then match.
    Result: The permanganate half consumes 5 e⁻ per MnO₄⁻ in this acidic reduction to Mn²⁺.
  2. Write Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ + e⁻ and multiply it by five so the electron count matches the permanganate half.
    Why valid: Electrons are bookkeeping intermediates and must cancel when the two half-reactions are added.
    Result: 5Fe²⁺ → 5Fe³⁺ + 5e⁻.
  3. Add the halves and verify MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5Fe²⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O + 5Fe³⁺ by separate atom and charge ledgers.
    Why valid: A valid ionic equation conserves every element and total electric charge.
    Result: Atoms balance and the total charge is +17 on both sides; the five-electron change is specific to this permanganate reaction in acid.

Equations and validity

EquationWhat it tells youWhen you can use it

total e− lost = total e− gained

Half-reaction balancing equalises electron transfer before the halves are added; the final ionic equation must also conserve each element and total charge.

  • Write separate oxidation and reduction half-reactions in the stated medium.
  • Balance atoms and charge within each half before scaling electron counts to cancel.

Watch one

Balance MnO₄⁻ + Fe²⁺ → Mn²⁺ + Fe³⁺ in acidic solution by the half-reaction method, then state the electron transfer associated with one MnO₄⁻ in this reaction.

  1. Balance MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O.
  2. Write Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ + e⁻ and multiply by five.
  3. Add the halves so 5e⁻ cancels.
  4. Check Mn, Fe, O and H atom counts and verify total charge +17 on each side.
  5. The corresponding 5-electron value belongs to this specified reaction and medium, not to permanganate under every condition.

Result

MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5Fe²⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O + 5Fe³⁺; one permanganate ion gains 5 electrons in this acidic reduction.

Check

The final equation must pass both the atom-count ledger and the total-charge ledger.

Half-reaction balancing is a conservation algorithm, while n-factor compresses the electron change for one specified redox transformation; neither is valid without the reaction context.

A tempting wrong path

  • Matching atom counts is enough even if charge differs, and every reagent has one universal n-factor.

Diagnose

  • Would an ionic equation with equal atom counts but unequal total charge be balanced, and can the same reagent form products at different oxidation states?

Repair

  • Keep separate atom and charge ledgers, and derive n-factor from the actual balanced transformation each time.

1-2-3 quick check

  1. 1 · CheckWhat two independent ledgers must balance after half-reaction electrons cancel?
  2. 2 · ApplyFor MnO₄⁻ + Fe²⁺ in acidic solution, determine the electron multiplier needed to combine the two half-reactions.
  3. 3 · ConnectAttach any n-factor you quote to this specific reaction and medium.
