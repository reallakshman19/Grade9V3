Lewis structures, formal charge and VSEPR geometry

Medium concept

The key step

Lewis structures conserve a declared valence-electron inventory, while VSEPR uses electron domains to predict geometry; neither bookkeeping convention should be promoted beyond its model scope.

You need

  • Can count valence electrons for simple main-group species and can distinguish a bonded atom position from a lone-pair electron domain.
  1. Count the total valence-electron inventory before placing bonds and lone pairs.
    Why valid: A Lewis structure that creates or loses electrons relative to the species charge is internally inconsistent.
    Result: Every candidate Lewis structure must preserve the electron count.
  2. Compute formal charges only after the Lewis arrangement is explicit.
    Why valid: Formal charge follows a defined partition of bonding electrons and is a diagnostic of the representation.
    Result: Formal charge helps compare contributors but is not a direct measured partial charge.
  3. Count VSEPR electron domains before naming molecular shape.
    Why valid: Lone pairs occupy domains and influence geometry even though they are omitted from the atom-position shape name.
    Result: Electron-domain geometry and molecular shape can differ.

Equations and validity

EquationWhat it tells youWhen you can use it

FC = V − N − B/2

Formal charge is Lewis electron bookkeeping for a chosen structure; VSEPR then uses electron-domain count, not formal charge, to name electron-domain geometry and molecular shape.

  • First draw a Lewis structure with the correct total valence-electron inventory.
  • Use the same declared Lewis bookkeeping convention for every atom compared.

Watch one

For NH₃, count the 8 valence electrons, build a Lewis structure, check the formal charge on N, then distinguish the electron-domain geometry from the molecular shape.

  1. N contributes 5 valence electrons and three H atoms contribute 3, giving 8 total.
  2. Three N–H single bonds use 6 electrons; the remaining pair is a lone pair on N.
  3. For N, FC = 5 − 2 − 6/2 = 0.
  4. Four domains give the tetrahedral electron-domain arrangement; omitting the lone-pair position from the atom-position name gives trigonal pyramidal molecular shape.

Result

NH₃ uses three N–H bonds and one lone pair on N; N has formal charge 0, four electron domains give tetrahedral electron-domain geometry, and the bonded-atom shape is trigonal pyramidal.

Check

Verify the 8-electron inventory, formal-charge sum, domain count and bonded-atom shape as four separate checks.

Lewis structures conserve a declared valence-electron inventory, while VSEPR uses electron domains to predict geometry; neither bookkeeping convention should be promoted beyond its model scope.

A tempting wrong path

  • A formal charge is the atom's measured charge, and a tetrahedral electron-domain geometry always means a tetrahedral molecular shape.

Diagnose

  • Can resonance contributors assign different formal charges to the same atom, and what happens to the shape name when one of four domains is a lone pair?

Repair

  • Treat formal charge as Lewis bookkeeping, then count electron domains and bonded-atom positions separately for VSEPR.

1-2-3 quick check

  1. 1 · CheckWhy is formal charge bookkeeping not the same thing as measured partial charge?
  2. 2 · ApplyNH₃ has four electron domains with one lone pair. Name its electron-domain geometry and molecular shape separately.
  3. 3 · ConnectUse the resulting geometry, not a bond count, when you next add molecular dipole vectors.

PCl5 geometry, model shorthand and molecular dipoles

The key step

Trigonal-bipyramidal geometry is a structural claim, whereas sp3d is simplified model language; molecular polarity then depends on vector addition in that geometry rather than a count of polar bonds.

You need

  • Can recognise a trigonal-bipyramidal arrangement and can add simple vectors qualitatively by symmetry.
  1. Place five electron domains in a trigonal-bipyramidal arrangement with three equatorial and two axial directions.
    Why valid: Axial and equatorial positions have different angular environments.
    Result: The geometry contains two distinct position classes.
  2. Qualify traditional sp3d terminology as model shorthand if it is used.
    Why valid: Modern bonding descriptions do not require literal d-orbital hybridisation to explain PCl5 geometry.
    Result: A course label must not be presented as experimental proof of orbital composition.
  3. Add bond dipoles as vectors in the molecular geometry.
    Why valid: Equal polar bonds can cancel by symmetry, so scalar bond counting cannot establish a net molecular dipole.
    Result: Geometry is indispensable to molecular-polarity prediction.

Equations and validity

EquationWhat it tells youWhen you can use it

μnet = Σ μ⃗bond

Molecular dipole is the vector sum of bond-dipole contributions in the molecular geometry; symmetric vectors can cancel even when individual bonds are polar.

  • Use the molecular geometry and the relevant bond-dipole directions.
  • For PCl₅ here, the trigonal-bipyramidal statement is the isolated-molecule/gas-phase model.

Watch one

For an isolated gaseous PCl₅ molecule in the trigonal-bipyramidal model, compare axial and equatorial angular environments and determine the net dipole for five equivalent P–Cl bond-dipole magnitudes by vector symmetry.

  1. Three equatorial P–Cl directions lie 120° apart in one plane.
  2. The two axial directions are 180° apart and each is 90° to every equatorial direction.
  3. Equal axial bond-dipole vectors cancel one another; the three equal equatorial vectors also sum to zero.
  4. This is an isolated-molecule/gas-phase structural model. It is not a description of solid PCl₅ and it does not prove literal d-orbital hybridisation.

Result

The two axial directions are opposite and cancel; the three equatorial directions are 120° apart and cancel, so the idealised symmetric isolated molecule has μnet = 0. Axial and equatorial sites are nevertheless geometrically inequivalent.

Check

State the phase/model boundary and perform the vector cancellation instead of inferring polarity from the presence of polar bonds.

Trigonal-bipyramidal geometry is a structural claim, whereas sp3d is simplified model language; molecular polarity then depends on vector addition in that geometry rather than a count of polar bonds.

A tempting wrong path

  • The label sp3d proves literal d-orbital bonding, and any molecule with polar bonds must have a nonzero net dipole.

Diagnose

  • Does a hybridisation label itself measure orbital composition, and can equal bond-dipole vectors cancel in a symmetric arrangement?

Repair

  • Separate geometry evidence from hybridisation shorthand and perform a vector-symmetry check before asserting molecular polarity.

1-2-3 quick check

  1. 1 · CheckState the axial-equatorial 90° relation and the equatorial-equatorial 120° relation in trigonal-bipyramidal PCl₅.
  2. 2 · ApplyFor isolated gaseous PCl₅ with five equivalent P–Cl bond-dipole magnitudes, use vector symmetry to determine μnet.
  3. 3 · ConnectKeep the isolated-molecule model boundary explicit before moving from geometry to orbital or bulk-property models.

Try it with less support

Molecular-orbital occupancy, magnetism and hydrogen bonding

Hard concept
Section 3 of 3← Previous

The key step

MO bond order and magnetism are separate consequences of the same orbital occupancy, while hydrogen bonding is a different intermolecular structural interaction requiring appropriate donor and acceptor sites.

You need

  • Can fill a supplied molecular-orbital ordering with electrons and can identify common hydrogen-bond donor and acceptor sites.
  1. Fill the stated molecular-orbital ordering with the correct electron count for the species.
    Why valid: Charge changes the electron inventory before either bond order or magnetism is read.
    Result: Occupancy is the common evidence base.
  2. Compute bond order from bonding-minus-antibonding occupancy and inspect unpaired electrons separately for magnetic behaviour.
    Why valid: Bond order and paramagnetism answer different questions even though both use the same configuration.
    Result: Do not infer one from the other.
  3. Identify hydrogen-bond donor and acceptor environments before using hydrogen bonding to explain a bulk property.
    Why valid: Merely containing hydrogen is not sufficient for the strong introductory hydrogen-bond interaction.
    Result: Bulk-property explanations should treat hydrogen bonding as one structural contribution among relevant intermolecular effects.

Equations and validity

EquationWhat it tells youWhen you can use it

BO = (Nb − Nab)/2

MO bond order follows bonding and antibonding occupancy, while magnetic behaviour requires a separate inspection of unpaired electrons.

  • Use a stated molecular-orbital ordering and the correct total electron count for the species.
  • Assess pairing after the occupancy is written.

Watch one

For O₂ in the usual second-row molecular-orbital ordering, use its 16-electron occupancy to determine bond order and magnetic behaviour. Keep the two readouts separate.

  1. Fill the molecular orbitals with the 16 electrons of neutral O₂ using the stated ordering and Hund's rule in the degenerate π* set.
  2. The bonding-minus-antibonding occupancy difference gives BO = 2.
  3. Two unpaired π* electrons remain, so the species is paramagnetic.
  4. Bond order and magnetism come from the same occupancy but answer different questions.

Result

O₂ has bond order 2 and is paramagnetic because two electrons occupy the degenerate π* antibonding orbitals singly.

Check

Show the bonding/antibonding electron count and the unpaired-electron count on separate lines.

MO bond order and magnetism are separate consequences of the same orbital occupancy, while hydrogen bonding is a different intermolecular structural interaction requiring appropriate donor and acceptor sites.

A tempting wrong path

  • Higher MO bond order guarantees diamagnetism, and every hydrogen-containing compound forms strong hydrogen bonds.

Diagnose

  • Can an MO configuration have positive bond order and an unpaired electron, and does a generic C-H bond meet the same donor condition as an O-H group in the usual introductory model?

Repair

  • Run the bond-order and paired/unpaired checks separately, then require an explicit donor/acceptor structure before invoking hydrogen bonding.

1-2-3 quick check

  1. 1 · CheckWhich occupancy count gives bond order, and which separate observation gives paramagnetism?
  2. 2 · ApplyFor O₂ in the usual second-row MO ordering, state BO and whether unpaired electrons are present.
  3. 3 · ConnectWhen explaining a bulk trend next, identify hydrogen-bond donors and acceptors rather than invoking hydrogen generically.
