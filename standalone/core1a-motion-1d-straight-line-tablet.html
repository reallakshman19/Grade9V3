Reference Frame, Distance and Displacement

Unit 1 · Pages 4–6
🎯 Learning Target: Separate path length from net change of position.
🧠 Memory Anchor
Distance follows the path. Displacement compares only start and end coordinates.

M-ST02B

Equal Time vs Equal Distance Averages

Unit 2 · Pages 13–15
🎯 Learning Target: Recognise whether time or distance is being weighted equally.
🧠 Memory Anchor
Equal times give equal time weights; equal distances give unequal times and a harmonic-type average.
⚙️ Why It Works
Equal times: (v₁ + v₂)/2. Equal distances: 2v₁v₂/(v₁ + v₂). If neither condition holds, return to total distance / total time.
🔍 Worked Contrast
10 and 15 m/s: equal times → 12.5 m/s; equal distances → 12 m/s.
✍️ Guided Practice & Active Recall
Example 1: A cyclist moves at 8 m/s for 3 min and then 12 m/s for the next 3 min. Find average speed. EQUAL TIME v₁ for T v₂ for T equal time weights → arithmetic mean

Core Principle: Equal times give equal time weights; equal distances give unequal times and a harmonic-type average.

EASY

A person travels distance × at velocity v₁ and then another equal distance × at velocity v₂ in the same direction. Relate the average velocity v to v₁ and v₂.

Notice: Both legs have the same distance x. Their times are x/v₁ and x/v₂.
Model: Total distance = 2x; total time = x/v₁ + x/v₂.
Start: vavg = 2x / (x/v₁ + x/v₂). Cancel × and simplify.
Reveal Method & Answer (Q1)

Why this works: Average speed or velocity must be built from total distance/displacement divided by total time. Different speeds contribute through the time spent at each speed, not by a blind arithmetic mean.

Method: Let each leg have distance x. The travel times are x/v₁ and x/v₂, so total distance = 2x and total time = x/v₁ + x/v₂. Therefore vavg = 2x/(x/v₁ + x/v₂) = 2v₁v₂/(v₁+v₂).

Answer / Check: v = 2v₁v₂/(v₁+v₂)

Concept to keep: Keep: average speed = total distance / total time. Equal-distance legs are time-weighted; for two equal distances this becomes the harmonic-mean form 2v₁v₂/(v₁+v₂).
