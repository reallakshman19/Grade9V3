⚖️ Newton's First Law: Motion is NOT Force

An object remains in a state of rest or uniform motion in a straight line unless acted upon by a non-zero net external resultant force.

The Core Mental Model:

Velocity does not require force to be maintained. A net force causes a CHANGE in velocity (acceleration $ ec{a} = rac{d ec{v}}{dt}$), NOT velocity itself! If $ ec{F}_{net} = 0$, then $ ec{a} = 0 \implies ec{v} = ext{constant}$.

Inertia and Mass

Inertia is the inherent tendency of an object to resist changes in its state of motion. Mass is the direct quantitative measure of inertia: a massive object resists acceleration more stubbornly than a light one under the same applied force.

Equilibrium Condition:   ∑ F_x = 0,   ∑ F_y = 0,   ∑ F_z = 0  ⇒  a = 0

📐 Free Body Diagram (FBD) Discipline

An FBD represents an isolated body or system as a point particle with all external contact and field forces drawn acting away from it.

Forces Checklist:
• Gravity (Weight): W = m · g (directed vertically downward)
• Normal Force (N): Contact force perpendicular to the interface
• Tension (T): Directed away from the body along the taut cord
• Friction (f): Opposes relative sliding (f_s ≤ μ_s N, f_k = μ_k N)
• External pushes/pulls (F_ext)
Golden Rule of FBD: Never include forces that the body exerts on other bodies. Only include forces exerted BY the environment ON the chosen body!

🚀 Newton's Second Law: Momentum & Impulse

The rate of change of linear momentum of a body is directly proportional to the applied net force and occurs in the direction of the force:

Linear Momentum:      p = m · v
Second Law:           F_net = dp / dt = m · (dv / dt) = m · a   (for constant mass)
Impulse-Momentum:    J = ∫ F dt = F_avg · Δt = Δp = m (v_f - v_i)

Catching Time & Impact Force

By pulling hands backward while catching a fast cricket ball, a player increases the impact time interval $\Delta t$. Because $\Delta p$ is fixed by the ball's incoming speed, increasing $\Delta t$ drastically reduces the average stopping force $ ec{F}_{avg} = rac{\Delta ec{p}}{\Delta t}$, preventing hand injury.

🔄 Newton's Third Law & Connected Systems

To every action there is always an equal and opposite reaction: $ ec{F}_{AB} = - ec{F}_{BA}$. Action-reaction pairs NEVER act on the same body, and therefore NEVER cancel each other!

Ideal Atwood Machine (Masses m₂ > m₁ over frictionless pulley):
  Acceleration:   a = (m₂ - m₁) / (m₁ + m₂) · g
  Tension:        T = (2 m₁ m₂) / (m₁ + m₂) · g

Two Blocks Connected on Smooth Surface (F pulls m₁ + m₂):
  a = F / (m₁ + m₂),   Contact Force / Cord Tension = m₂ · a
